# Embodied Interaction Experiments

A series of personal experiments in embodied interaction and physical computing, developed with Arduino during 2023–2024.

These projects explore how invisible human experiences — uncertainty, social perception, emotion, stress, boundaries, avoidance, and judgment — can be translated into physical interactions through sensing, computation, and feedback.

## Research Through Making

Rather than treating Arduino as an end in itself, these experiments use physical computing as a medium for exploring relationships between:

* human experience and physical space
* sensing and perception
* internal states and external signals
* uncertainty and interaction
* human input and machine response

A recurring structure across the projects is:

```text
Human experience
       ↓
      Input
       ↓
     Sensing
       ↓
   State / Logic
       ↓
 Physical response
       ↓
   Interaction
```

## Technical Exploration

Across the experiments, different sensing and output mechanisms were tested, including:

**Sensors**

* Ultrasonic distance sensors
* PIR motion sensors
* Microwave radar sensors
* Touch sensors
* Temperature sensors
* Flex sensors
* Physical switches

**Outputs**

* Servo motors
* LEDs
* OLED displays
* Piezo buzzers
* Mechanical movement
* Light intensity
* Sound

This progression allowed me to experiment with different ways of translating physical input into machine-readable states and then back into perceptible physical feedback.

## Looking Back

Looking back, these experiments were an early exploration of a question that continued to evolve:

> **How can invisible states be made observable through interaction?**

At the time, the experiments focused primarily on physical and social experience. Later work moved toward human–AI interaction and cognitive systems, where the same basic question appears in a different form:

```text
Physical sensing
      ↓
Interaction states
      ↓
Human–AI interaction
      ↓
Cognitive states
```

The work therefore represents an early stage in an ongoing exploration of interaction, sensing, state, and human–machine relationships.
